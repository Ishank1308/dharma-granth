# Modern-context notes: Brahma Sutra (review note, 21 November 2026)

This note goes with the pull request that adds modern-context notes to the Brahma Sutra. It lists how the notes were checked, the sutras a reviewer should read first, and the facts to spot-check. The notes are AI drafts and have not had a human editorial review.

## Status

- **Coverage so far:** sutras 1.1.6 to 3.4.13 (440 notes). The first five sutras already had notes. 117 sutras remain, from 3.4.14 to 4.4.22.
- **Source file:** `data/hi-commentary/brahmasutra-rest.json` holds only the `lifeLesson` field. The existing व्याख्या (`explanation`) is unchanged for all 585 entries; this was verified after publishing.
- **Shape:** each note has आधुनिक संदर्भ (one real-world fact linked to the sutra), आज के जीवन में प्रयोग (three applications) and चिंतन प्रश्न (one question).

## How the notes were checked

- **Register:** every note passed `scripts/check-hindi-register.mjs` with no sentences to fix. The words are tatsam with no Urdu, Persian or English beyond unavoidable proper names and scientific terms (for example हाइड्रोजन, इलेक्ट्रॉन, प्रोटीन). There is no first person; first-person source lines are paraphrased in the third person.
- **Facts used once:** each batch was compared with every earlier note in all books by shared rare words and by shared years. Repeats found by these checks were replaced before publishing (thirty-two of them in sutras 1.2.16 to 3.4.13).
- **Anchoring:** each note states the adhikarana (topic) the sutra belongs to, then says in one sentence what the sutra itself teaches, following the existing व्याख्या.

## Please read these places first

- **Eligibility by birth (apaśūdra-adhikaraṇa, 1.3.34–1.3.38):** every note carries the line that this section reflects an old social order, is not a model for today, and that keeping anyone from knowledge, education or practice by birth is an injustice. The facts chosen point the other way: the Heliodorus pillar (1.3.35), the 2017 appointment of non-Brahmin priests in Kerala (1.3.36), the Vajrasuchika Upanishad's rejection of birth as the test (1.3.37) and the Right to Education Act (1.3.38). This mirrors the caveats in the existing व्याख्या.
- **Gods' eligibility and the eternity of the Veda (1.3.26–1.3.33):** each note says this is a discussion inside the scripture's own language of faith. The note at 1.3.29 says plainly that the eternity of the Veda is the tradition's belief and that historians date the texts by their own methods.
- **Other schools (1.1.6–1.1.11, 1.4.1–1.4.14, 2.1.1–2.1.13, 2.2.1–2.2.45, 2.3.51–2.3.53):** every note on a sutra that argues against Sankhya, Yoga, Vaisheshika, Buddhist, Jain, Pashupata or Pancharatra positions carries a line that this is classical debate between schools, that each school's teachers explain their view in their own way, and that the aim is to make the Vedanta argument clear, not to slight any tradition.
  - Buddhist section (2.2.18–2.2.32): the note at 2.2.32 adds Nagarjuna's own reading of emptiness as dependent arising, and notes that Advaita itself was called "pracchanna bauddha" by its critics.
  - Jain section (2.2.33): the note opens with Haribhadra Suri's verse on taking whichever statement is reasonable, and gives the Jain reply that the opposite predicates are said from different standpoints.
  - Jain view of the soul's size (2.2.34–2.2.36): each note adds that the Jain idea of a soul in every creature is the ground of their ideal of non-violence; 2.2.34 opens with the monks' peacock-feather whisk.
  - Pashupata and other Shaiva teachers (2.2.37–2.2.41): each note says the difference is over doctrine only, not over devotion to Shiva. The note at 2.2.37 opens with the Dakshinamurti Stotra attributed to Shankara.
  - Pancharatra (2.2.42–2.2.45): each note says Shankara accepts the Agama's devotion and worship and objects only to the statement that the soul is produced, and that Ramanuja reads the same sutras in support of Pancharatra. The facts are respectful ones: worship at Srirangam follows the Pancharatra Agama (2.2.42) and the Vaikuntha image at Khajuraho (2.2.44).
  - Vaisheshika (2.2.17): the sutra says the view may be disregarded; the note limits this to the question of the world's cause and cites Kanada's contribution with respect.
- **Karma, inequality and suffering (2.1.35–2.1.37):** each note says karma and rebirth are the scripture's faith, that the doctrine here explains God's impartiality, and that it must never be used to blame a suffering, ill or poor person; compassion and service are equally dharma.
- **Order of the elements (2.3.1–2.3.15):** each note says this is the Upanishads' own view of creation and not a description of modern physics. Notes on objection sutras (2.3.1, 2.3.3–2.3.5) say "पूर्वपक्ष रखता है".
- **Size of the individual self (2.3.20–2.3.28):** these sutras state the objector's view that the self is atomic. Each note says so, says Shankara's own conclusion comes later, and says teachers such as Ramanuja hold the atomic view. Each also says that leaving the body and travelling to other worlds are faith and symbolic language. The notes at 2.3.29–2.3.32 give Shankara's conclusion and say that Ramanuja reads these sutras differently.
- **Birth and social standing (2.3.43, 2.3.48):** the note at 2.3.43 opens with Kanakadasa's window at Udupi and says plainly that keeping anyone out by birth is an injustice. The note at 2.3.48 (rules that differ with the body) says such rules look at the body's condition and do not rank anyone, and its second application says a difference in rules must never become a difference in respect.
- **The journey after death (3.1.1–3.1.29):** every note says these descriptions are faith and symbolic language, not established fact.
  - 3.1.8–3.1.12: each note adds that the Upanishad's sentence tying the result of action to birth in a family or species reflects an old social order, and that ranking anyone by birth is an injustice.
  - 3.1.13–3.1.17 (Yama and the hells): each note says this is the language of faith and moral teaching, meant to recall that actions have results and not to frighten.
  - 3.1.18–3.1.23 (kinds of birth): each note says this is the old period's own classification, not modern biology.
  - 3.1.27 (animal sacrifice in old rites): the note opens with Ashoka's first rock edict against killing animals for offerings, and says it does not support sacrifice or harm to any creature.
- **Dream, deep sleep and fainting (3.2.1–3.2.10):** the note at 3.2.4 says dream omens are a traditional belief and that no fear or large decision should rest on a dream alone. Notes on deep sleep and fainting (3.2.7–3.2.10) say the channels and the heart are the Upanishad's own language, not modern physiology. The note at 3.2.10 says a person who faints needs a doctor at once.
- **With and without attributes (3.2.11–3.2.21):** every note says Shankara takes the attributeless nature as primary, that teachers such as Ramanuja hold Brahman to have endless auspicious qualities, and that both honour worship with form. The note at 3.2.17 sets Maimonides's way of negation beside the sutra and says the comparison is of method only.
- **"Not this, not this" and the relation of self and Brahman (3.2.22–3.2.31):** every note says the Vedanta traditions differ on difference and non-difference. The note at 3.2.27 paraphrases a well-known sakhi of Kabir (drop and ocean) without quoting it. The note at 3.2.30 names Nimbarka's commentary and the other acharyas with equal respect.
- **Who gives the fruit of action (3.2.39–3.2.42):** every note names Jaimini's and Badarayana's views with respect, says the doctrine of karma and its fruit is the scripture's faith, and says it must not be used to blame a suffering person.
- **Combining meditations (3.3.1–3.3.41):** each note names its adhikarana and says whether the sutra is the objector's view (3.3.7, 3.3.21, 3.3.41).
  - 3.3.3–3.3.4 (the Atharvavedins' vow): the notes treat it as one group's rule of study and ask for respect for another tradition's vows.
  - 3.3.19 (sipping water before and after food): the note says this is the language of meditation, not health advice.
  - 3.3.26 (opening mantras that pray to pierce an enemy): the note says such mantras belong to old ritual, are not part of the knowledge of Brahman, and that the note supports harm to no one.
  - 3.3.27 (others receiving the knower's merit and demerit): the note says Shankara reads this as praise of knowledge, not a literal transfer and not a punishment for anyone.
  - 3.3.27–3.3.32 (merit, demerit and the path of the gods): every note says descriptions of what follows death are faith and symbolic language. Notes at 3.3.30–3.3.31 add that Shankara denies any journey for the knower of the attributeless Brahman and that other teachers read this differently.
  - 3.3.33 (sages who return for a task): the note says this is the tradition's belief, as told in the Puranas.
  - 3.3.41 (offering the first mouthfuls to the breaths, even on a fast day): the note says this is the objector's view, that the next sutra answers it, and that fasting or not is a personal decision made with one's health in mind.
- **Mental fires (3.3.45–3.3.54):** each note names the question (part of the rite, or independent meditation) and marks the objector's sutras (3.3.46, 3.3.47).
- **Is the self other than the body (3.3.55–3.3.56):** both notes call the Lokayata (materialist) view an old and respected tradition of Indian philosophy, call the section a classical debate, and say the nature of consciousness is still an open question in science and philosophy. The note at 3.3.55 gives the materialist argument its full force.
- **Meditations tied to parts of a rite (3.3.57–3.3.58, 3.3.63–3.3.68):** objector's sutras (3.3.63–3.3.66) are marked as such. The note at 3.3.64 uses the helmet law only as an example of a binding rule.
- **Knowledge and action (3.4.1–3.4.13):** every note names Badarayana's and Jaimini's positions and says both traditions deserve respect; sutras 3.4.2–3.4.7 are marked as Jaimini's view. Notes at 3.4.5 and 3.4.11 add that descriptions of what follows death are faith and symbolic language. The note at 3.4.10 says medicines should be taken only on a doctor's advice.
- **After-death paths and states (1.2.16, 1.3.42, 1.4.22):** each note says these are faith and symbolic language, not established fact.
- **Science parallels:** wherever a modern finding is set beside a sutra, the note says "यह साम्य केवल रूपक है" (the likeness is only a metaphor).
- **Teachers' differing views (1.2.28–1.2.31, 1.4.20–1.4.23):** Jaimini, Ashmarathya, Audulomi, Kashakritsna and Badari are each named with respect; the notes do not rank living traditions that follow different readings.
