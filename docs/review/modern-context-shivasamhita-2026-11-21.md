# Modern-context notes: Shiva Samhita (review note, 21 November 2026)

This note goes with the pull request that adds modern-context notes to the Shiva Samhita. It lists how the notes were checked, the verses a reviewer should read first, and the facts to spot-check. The notes are AI drafts and have not had a human editorial review.

## Status

- **Coverage:** all 644 verses of the book (1:1 to 5:255) now have a modern-context note. None had one before.
- **Source file:** `data/hi-commentary/shivasamhita-rest.json` is new and holds only the `lifeLesson` field. The existing व्याख्या (`explanation`) is unchanged for all 644 entries; this is verified after each publish.
- **Shape:** each note has आधुनिक संदर्भ (one real-world fact linked to the verse), आज के जीवन में प्रयोग (three applications) and चिंतन प्रश्न (one question).

## How the notes were checked

- **Register:** every note passed `scripts/check-hindi-register.mjs` with no sentences to fix. The words are tatsam with no Urdu, Persian or English beyond unavoidable proper names and scientific terms (for example हाइड्रोजन, हीलियम, टेफ्लॉन). There is no first person. Where Shiva speaks in the first person (1:2, 1:3, 1:33, 1:34), the note reports it in the third person.
- **Facts used once:** candidate facts were screened by keyword against every earlier note in all books before writing. A stricter check (rare words or years shared by any two notes) was meant to run on each batch, but for this book it did not read the Shiva Samhita files, so it reported nothing. The check was corrected and run over all 644 notes at the end. It found 13 facts that an earlier note had already used (2:43, 3:6, 3:13, 3:56, 4:18, 4:23, 4:64, 5:2, 5:24, 5:174, 5:195, 5:197, 5:198) and one sentence that overlapped an earlier note (5:126). All 14 now carry a fresh fact and passed the checks again. Notes that share only a year (different events of the same year) or a body part (the spinal-cord notes at 2:6, 2:16, 2:18 and 2:30; the hip and knee notes at 3:106, 3:117 and 4:37) were left as they are; the caution about the knee is repeated on purpose.
- **Anchoring:** each note says in one sentence what the verse itself teaches, following the existing व्याख्या.

## Please read these places first

- **The list of other views (1:4–1:16):** the text lists many paths and schools and calls them confusing. Every note in this range carries a line that these are respected traditions of Indian thought and that the text lists them as a lead-in to its own path of yoga, not to censure anyone.
  - 1:11 (those who accept only what is perceived): the note opens with Jayarashi's Tattvopaplavasimha and treats the demand for evidence with respect.
  - 1:13 (the text calls these thinkers "averse to the highest goal"): the note reports this as the text's own wording.
  - 1:15 (the text says these sages confuse people): the note says this is the text's own style and that all of them are respected teachers.
- **Praise of the text's own teaching (1:17–1:18):** each note says such praise is an old textual style (arthavāda) and does not lower other scriptures.
- **Secrecy (1:19):** the note says secrecy here means fitness and guidance, not keeping any group out. Its first application says prāṇāyāma and difficult practices should be done only under a qualified teacher.
- **Heaven, hell and rebirth (1:9, 1:23–1:28):** each note says these descriptions are the scripture's faith and symbolic language, not established fact.
  - 1:26 (merit brings happiness, sin brings sorrow): the note adds that nobody's suffering should be judged as the fruit of their sin, and its third application says to help a suffering person, not lecture them.
  - 1:28: the printed meaning gives "others' wives" as an example of what is envied in heaven. The note does not repeat this and speaks only of others' splendour and happiness.
- **Giving up ritual action (1:30–1:31):** each note says giving up means dropping attachment to the fruit, not leaving family or social duty. The note at 1:31 adds that the text speaks to a seeker who already knows the worth of ritual.
- **Shiva as the mover of the mind in both sin and merit (1:33):** the note adds that responsibility for an act stays with the person who does it.
- **The world as appearance (1:35–1:49, 1:59, 1:67):** each note says this is the Advaita view and that other schools explain the same point in their own way. The notes at 1:39, 1:59 and 1:67 add that calling the world unreal does not mean giving up everyday duty. The note at 1:49 adds that "imagined" does not mean "useless".
- **1:41:** the existing व्याख्या says the reading of this verse is unclear. The note says "as far as the reading can be understood".
- **Gods as impermanent (1:45):** the note says this is the Advaita view in which every name and form falls within māyā, that the devotional schools hold God to be eternal, and that both views deserve respect.
- **Consciousness as the source of the world (1:50):** the note says neuroscience studies consciousness together with brain activity and that calling consciousness the root of everything is the scripture's view.
- **Samādhi (1:65–1:66):** the note at 1:66 says samādhi belongs to the experience of practitioners and to scripture, and that research only measures outward signs that go with it. Both notes send the reader to an experienced teacher for meditation practice.
- **Dispassion toward body, wealth and pleasure (1:68):** the note says dispassion means lessening attachment, not neglecting the body or family duty. Its third application says to give the body proper food and rest.
- **Creation and the elements (1:74–1:80, 1:83, 1:90, 1:97–1:99):** each note says the order of creation and the pairing of elements with qualities are philosophy's own language and classification, not a statement of modern physics.
- **Gunas paired with the Goddess, Vishnu and Shiva (1:86–1:88):** each note says this pairing is the text's own philosophical scheme, that no deity is ranked above or below another, and that the Shakta, Vaishnava and Shaiva traditions each hold their own chosen deity as the highest reality. The note at 1:89 opens with the Smarta pañcāyatana worship.
- **"Freedom from death" (1:93):** the note says this means freedom from the fear and grief of death, not a promise that the body will not die.
- **The body as sorrow (1:95–1:96):** each note says this is a style meant to awaken dispassion, that the same text later calls the body the base of practice, and that neglect of the body is not meant.
- **Union of Shiva and Shakti (1:98):** the note says this is the symbolic language of tantra. Its third application asks for equal respect for women and men.
- **Karma and rebirth (1:95, 1:100–1:102, 2:39–2:44, 2:46, 2:52):** each note says karma and rebirth are matters of the scripture's faith and that nobody's suffering may be called their own fault because of them.
- **The body as a small cosmos (2:1–2:6, 2:9–2:12, 2:37–2:38):** each note says this description is the symbolic language of yoga, not a statement of anatomy. The facts from anatomy and cell biology are marked as a likeness only.
- **Nadis (2:7–2:8, 2:13–2:20, 2:25–2:26, 2:28–2:32):** each note says nadis belong to the subtle body of the yoga tradition and are not to be equated with nerves or blood vessels. The note at 2:30 tells the reader to go to a hospital at once for chest pain.
- **Muladhara and kundalini (2:21–2:24, 2:27, 3:1):** each note says chakras and kundalini are the yoga tradition's language of meditation, not anatomy, and that related practices should be learned only from an experienced teacher. The note at 2:21 gives the place of the muladhara in plain words ("the lowest part of the trunk"). The note at 2:22 says the word "yoni" in the verse means source and the triangle symbol, following the existing व्याख्या.
- **2:25:** the existing व्याख्या records that this verse sends the ida nadi to the right nostril, unlike most yoga texts. The note says so and does not correct the verse.
- **Digestive fire (2:33–2:36):** each note says this is the language of yoga and Ayurveda and that a qualified doctor should be consulted in illness.
- **2:43 and 2:44:** the existing व्याख्या calls both verses unclear, and each note says so.
- **Varna and ashrama (2:54):** the note says the mention belongs to the social order of an older time, that its lesson today is to do one's duty without desire for the fruit, and that holding anyone high or low by birth is unjust.
- **Harsh words for pleasure-seekers (2:55):** the note says the harsh tone is a style of warning and is not meant to hold any person low.
- **Giving up action (2:56):** the note says this applies only to one who has already reached the goal, and its first application says not to drop one's rules and duties until the mind is steady.
- **The ten pranas (3:2–3:8):** each note says the description is the language of yoga and Ayurveda, not a statement of modern physiology. The facts from physiology are marked as a likeness only.
- **Knowing the body as cosmos frees from sin (3:9):** the note says this is a way of praising knowledge and that responsibility for what one has done remains.
- **The guru (3:11–3:15):** each note says this reverence is the tradition's style, that it does not mean giving up one's own judgement, and that it is right to leave a guide who exploits or harms. The third application at 3:13 says to keep one's judgement awake and to question what is improper.
- **Who does and does not reach success (3:16–3:20):** the notes at 3:16, 3:19 and 3:20 say the description of success and its signs is the text's faith and style, not a promise of a fixed result. The notes at 3:17 and 3:18 say the lists are for the seeker's own self-examination, not for judging others, and the note at 3:17 says the point is to lessen entanglement, not to drop relationships.
- **Pranayama and breath retention (3:22, 3:24–3:27, 3:46–3:48, 3:50, 3:62, 3:67, 3:69):** the notes at 3:22, 3:24–3:27, 3:48 and 3:50 say the method is the text's description, that retention should be learned only under an experienced teacher, and that anyone with heart, breathing or blood-pressure illness should ask a doctor first. The notes at 3:46, 3:47, 3:62, 3:67 and 3:69 say that the long retentions named in the text belong to the tradition, that forcing the breath can harm, and that no such practice should be tried alone.
  - 3:27 (four sessions a day, including midnight): the note says this schedule is for seekers living in seclusion and that a householder should not cut sleep.
  - 3:50 (trembling, hopping, moving in the sky): the note opens with the medical signs of over-breathing and says trembling is a signal to stop, not an attainment.
  - 3:62 (retention for three ghatikas): the note says even lifelong divers hold the breath for minutes only, and its second application says never to try breath-holding alone or in water.
- **Signs and fruits of practice (3:28–3:34, 3:52–3:53):** each note says these are the text's faith and style. The note at 3:52 says forcing sleep down is harmful. The note at 3:53 says sweat and saliva are healthy functions of the body.
- **Rubbing sweat into the body (3:49):** the note says this is a belief of the tradition and gives today's advice: replace water and salt and keep the body clean.
- **Diet and conduct rules (3:36–3:38, 3:40, 3:43–3:45, 3:54):** the notes at 3:36, 3:40, 3:44, 3:45 and 3:54 say the rules were stated for secluded seekers of that time and that a large change of diet needs a doctor or dietitian.
  - 3:37 lists fasting among the obstacles. The note says the verse means fasting that torments the body, not censure of a vow kept in faith.
  - 3:38 lists "company of women". The note says the list addresses the male seekers of that time, that the sense is restraint of lust and not censure of women, and that the same restraint applies to every seeker.
  - 3:40 lists betel. The note says betel with areca nut is held harmful by medicine today and should not be taken from this list.
  - 3:43 ties meals and sleep to the nostril in flow. The note says this is a rule of the svarodaya tradition, not of medicine.
- **Secrecy (3:39):** the note says secrecy means fitness and guidance, not keeping anyone out.
- **"Even if the life-breath comes to the throat" (3:56):** the note says this is a way of stressing firmness and does not mean putting health or life at risk.
- **Pranayama destroying karma, sin and merit (3:58–3:60):** the notes at 3:58 and 3:59 say karma and rebirth are matters of faith. The note at 3:59 says the end of merit means dropping attachment to its fruit, not dropping good deeds. The note at 3:60 says a wrong done still needs to be set right.
- **Siddhis: levitation, the eight powers, entering another body, gold, invisibility, standing on the thumb, many bodies (3:51, 3:55, 3:61, 3:63–3:65, 3:70, 3:74):** each note says these statements are the faith and style of the hatha-yoga tradition and should not be taken as established fact or as the aim of practice. Applications warn against anyone who shows wonders to ask for money or obedience (3:61) or promises wealth through practice (3:64).
- **Chakras and the five dharanas (3:72, 3:75–3:77):** each note carries the chakra caveat. The note at 3:76 says the two-hour periods belong to the tradition.
- **No death "even when a hundred Brahmas die" (3:78):** the note says this is hyperbole that points to the self the tradition holds to be beyond birth and death, not to an immortal body. Its third application warns against those who promise immortality or lasting youth.
- **Samadhi and the liberated person (3:80–3:81):** each note carries the samadhi caveat. The note at 3:80 gives a householder example.
- **Verses the व्याख्या calls unclear (3:54, 3:69, 3:73):** each note says so.
- **Cure of disease (3:83–3:84, 3:86, 3:89–3:90, 3:92, 3:109, 3:113, 3:118, 4:33):** each note says these statements are the tradition's faith and that a qualified doctor's treatment must be taken in any illness.
  - 3:83: the note opens with the 2016 Cochrane review, which found that yoga may bring a small improvement in asthma and does not replace medicine.
  - 3:89 (tuberculosis): the note opens with the history of treatment and says the doctor's medicine must be taken for the full course and that no breathing method replaces it.
- **Conquest of death and old age (3:87, 3:91, 3:93, 3:95–3:96, 4:19, 4:33):** each note says this is the tradition's style and means freedom from the fear of death, not an immortal body. The note at 3:87 adds that turning the tongue upward should not be tried on one's own.
- **Siddhis: becoming a poet, far hearing and sight, "a second god of love", free movement, every wish (3:88, 3:90, 3:94, 3:97–3:98, 3:115, 4:8, 4:13, 4:19):** each note says these statements are the faith and style of the hatha tradition, not proven fact and not the goal of practice. The notes at 3:103, 4:17, 4:18, 4:26, 4:34 and 4:42 say the description of success is the text's faith and style, not a promise of a fixed result.
- **An unclear term (3:88):** the note says the meaning of 'राजदन्त के छिद्र' is unclear, as the existing व्याख्या does.
- **Rebirth and the world of the gods (3:99):** the note says these are matters of the scripture's faith and symbolic language, not established fact.
- **Asanas (3:101–3:102, 3:104, 3:106, 3:112, 3:116–3:117, 4:28, 4:37):** each note says an asana is done within the body's limit and without force, and that a doctor or trained teacher should be asked first when the knees, lower back or spine give trouble.
  - 3:101 (siddhasana), 4:27 and 4:38: the placing of the heel and the contraction are described with the plain phrase "the base of the trunk"; the anatomical words of the verse are not repeated.
- **Pranayama inside the asana verses (3:85, 3:107–3:108, 3:110–3:111):** each note carries the pranayama caveat (learn retention only under an experienced guru; see a doctor first with heart, breath or blood-pressure illness).

The note at 3:105 adds that responsibility for what one has done remains. The note at 4:35 opens with a navigator who taught a guarded art to outsiders when it was dying out, and its third application says secrecy must not become the reason a body of knowledge is lost.

The note at 4:2 says 'yoni' here means source and that the 'kama-bija' is a mantra symbol for meditation. At 4:6 the verse has Shiva speaking in the first person; the note reports it in the third person.
- **Defective mantras (4:9–4:12, 4:14):** each note says this discussion is the tantra tradition's own language and that mantra practice is learned only from the guru who gives initiation.
- **Any mantra, "auspicious or inauspicious" (4:13):** the note carries the siddhi caveat, and its second application says the power of the word is to be used only for a good purpose.
- **No taint of sin even after the gravest crimes (4:15–4:16):** each note says the statement is hyperbole in praise of the mudra, that it is not permission for any wrong act, and that responsibility for one's acts always remains. The note at 4:15 opens with a 2009 experiment on how feeling virtuous makes people careless; the note at 4:16 opens with Angulimala, who still bore the result of his deeds after he changed. Please read these two closely.
- **Kundalini and the fruits of the mudras (4:22–4:23, 4:32, 4:41):** each note carries the chakra caveat. The note at 4:41 says its evidence for stronger bones concerns load-bearing exercise, not the bandha.
- **The ten mudras, mahamudra and mahabandha (4:24–4:25, 4:27–4:30, 4:38–4:39):** each note says these practices are the text's description, that they are to be learned only under an experienced teacher and not from a book, and that a doctor should be asked first in illness or pregnancy. The third application at 4:39 says not to try alone any practice that forcibly holds the breath or the body.
- **Nadis (3:114, 4:31, 4:40):** each note carries the nadi caveat.
- **"No need to deliberate" (4:34):** the third application says to read this as the language of faith and to keep testing one's own experience.
- **Vajroli and the verses that follow it (4:78–4:87, 4:94–4:98, 4:101–4:103):** these are the most sensitive verses in the book. No note repeats the method. Each note says the method is the text's description, that it harms the body and must not be imitated, and that free consent and respect on both sides are required in every relationship. Please read all of them.
  - 4:81: the note says the verse treats a woman as a means to someone's practice and that this view cannot be accepted today.
  - 4:84: the note opens with the infection risk of urinary catheters even in hospital, and its first application says never to put any object or fluid into an opening of the body.

  - 4:103 ("a hundred women"): the note does not repeat the phrase. It says the verse counts women as objects and that this view cannot be accepted today, and it opens with the 2013 definition of consent in Indian law.
  - 4:79, 4:80, 4:94 (liberation while living without rules or in enjoyment): the notes say truth, non-violence and restraint are not set aside by any method. The third application at 4:80 says to keep away from, and report, anyone who exploits in the name of practice.
- **Retention of bindu (4:88–4:91, 4:104):** each note says these statements are the faith and symbolic language of hatha yoga, that medicine today does not accept them, and that nobody should feel fear or guilt because of them. - **Jalandhara, mula bandha and uddiyana (4:60, 4:64–4:66, 4:68, 4:72–4:73):** each note carries the mudra caveat (teacher only, doctor first in illness or pregnancy). - **Unclear term (5:51):** the note says the meaning of "vijnana-nadi" is unclear, as the व्याख्या does, and says never to press on the neck.

- **Conquering death (5:68, 5:90) and health claims (5:87):** the usual caveats. 5:90 adds that a guru's word, too, is to be tested by one's own judgement and experience.
