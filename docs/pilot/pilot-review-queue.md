# Pilot review queue

Everything below needs a person. Nothing here was fixed silently.

## Canonical problems (protected fields: not edited)

1. **Ramcharitmanas (3 units, review-only).** The stored `sanskrit` field mixes the original with a Hindi gloss, and `translation`, `hindi` and `wordMeaning` hold unrelated or repeated lines. No draft was written. Needs a fresh, verified source for the text.
2. **Chandogya 6.8.7 transliteration.** The stored IAST field is not IAST (it has Harvard-Kyoto style artefacts such as `{\m+}`). The field is protected, so it was left as is.
3. **Devi Mahatmya Devi Stuti block.** The stored record merges 22 separate stanzas under one number (labelled 5.77), and the standard "citirupena" stanza appears to be missing before the next record (5.81). Needs checking against an edition and, if confirmed, a canonical correction.
4. **Brihadaranyaka (not selected).** "अहं ब्रह्मास्मि" is stored as a whole verse in several positions (1:1, 2:4, 3:5, 4:4, 5:4).
5. **Vishnu Purana references.** The dataset uses a running chapter index and its own verse numbering; the edition numbers of the three pilot units are unverified. Hindi and English meanings in these units belonged to other verses.
6. **Chandogya, Katha, Devi and Vishnu Purana chapter numbers** in the dataset are running indexes, not the texts' own chapter numbers (the pilot labels use the text's own).

## Unresolved scholarly questions, by unit

- **Bhagavad Gita 2.47** (confidence high): The verse is clear. 'karma-phala-hetu' has two readings and the Vyakhya names both without choosing. The stored Hindi adds 'kartavya' (duty), which is not in the Sanskrit; the revision removes it. Not checked against classical commentaries: none are stored in the repository.
- **Bhagavad Gita 2.14** (confidence high): The stored explanation says suffering 'is not caused by external events but by our reaction to them'. The verse does not say this; it says sense-contacts give pleasure and pain, are impermanent, and are to be borne. That sentence was not carried into the revision. The stored Hindi adds parenthetical glosses ('jad padarth', 'anukulta', 'pratikulta') that are not in the Sanskrit; they were removed. No classical commentary is stored in the repository.
- **Bhagavad Gita 2.20** (confidence medium): The Sanskrit is clear. The phrase 'bhutva bhavita va na bhuyah' can be parsed two ways and the stored Hindi and English follow different parsings, so they disagree; the revision keeps one neutral rendering and the Vyakhya names both. Vijnana is left empty on purpose: a statement about the unborn Self is metaphysical and any physics or biology parallel would be forced. The Kathopanishad parallel (1.2.18) is mentioned from general knowledge and should be checked against the Katha record. No classical commentary is stored.
- **Bhagavad Gita 18.66** (confidence medium): The Sanskrit is clear but the verse is interpreted differently by different schools. The Vyakhya describes the two broad directions in general terms and does not attribute them to any named commentator, because no commentary text is stored in the repository; these descriptions come from general knowledge and must be checked against primary commentaries by a reviewer from each tradition. The stored word-gloss renders 'sucah' as 'do not fear'; the word means 'grieve'. Vijnana is left empty on purpose: surrender is devotional and theological, and a psychological parallel would flatten it.
- **Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7** (confidence medium): The Sanskrit text is intact. Three stored fields are damaged and were not repaired: (1) the transliteration field is not IAST (it contains Harvard-Kyoto style artefacts such as '{\m+}'); it is a protected canonical field, so it is routed to canonical review rather than edited. (2) The stored Hindi is OCR noise unrelated to this verse (it talks about a thief), so it was replaced. (3) The stored English is an old public-domain style translation with no attribution in the data; it was not reproduced. The dataset's chapter number 105 is a running index; the Upanishad's own reference is 6.8.7. The two readings of 'tat tvam asi' are described in general terms only; no commentary is stored.
- **Isha Upanishad 1** (confidence medium): The Sanskrit is clear but 'vasyam', 'tena tyaktena' and 'kasya svid dhanam' each admit more than one reading; the Artha keeps one neutral rendering and the Vyakhya names the alternatives.
